Vision

Where this goes, and why I believe it works.

The short version: giving spreads on its own when people can see it. Proof is what makes it stick. So the unit of Glimpse isn't the donation. It's the receipt.

The trust gap
$592.5B
Given to charity in the US in 2024.Giving USA 2025.
19%
Of first-time donors give a second time. Four out of five never come back.Fundraising Effectiveness Project, 2024 panel. Source.

Not because they stopped caring. Because nobody showed them what happened.

My Monolith hackathon deck, March 2026. Still the whole problem in one line.
Giving is viral

This isn't a hope. It's the most repeatable thing in the whole sector.

$220M
Raised globally by the Ice Bucket Challenge in about three weeks of summer 2014, from 17 million videos.Source.
3×
GoFundMe campaigns shared on social raise three times more. One share adds about $13.GoFundMe's own data. Source.
$41M
TeamWater, 2025: 100,000+ donors, $12M of it in a single livestream.Source.
$4.0B
GivingTuesday 2025, in one day, from 38 million people.Source.

A visible act of giving makes the next one. That's the whole mechanism, and it works whether or not anyone builds a product around it.

Proof is what makes it stick
2025
A peer-reviewed study across 1,900 people: showing donors the actual impact of their gift raised both the willingness to give again and the size of the repeat gift. Anticipated impact didn't.Journal of Experimental Social Psychology. Source.
171,000+
Projects charity: water has funded with a GPS pin and a photo on every one. The closest working model to posted, funded, bought, proven.Source.
$500K
What the Ice Bucket Challenge raised when it was rerun in 2015, against $78 million in the same weeks a year earlier. Attention without a new proof event dies in a year.Source.

So I don't run campaigns. I run receipts. Every need is its own proof event, and the next one starts the day the photo lands.

The flywheel

In chapter one the distribution was a phone: 116,000 Seekers in 50 countries. In chapter two the distribution is X, the biggest social app there is, and Paid is the rail that turns attention into dollars without me touching a wallet.

01
A trade on $GIVE leaves a fee. Small, automatic, every time.
02
The fee buys a need. One nonprofit, one item under $500, named in advance.
03
The receipt is posted. Posted, funded, bought, proven, with the photo.
04
The receipt is the content. A real thing, bought for a real org, with proof. That's what people share.
05
The content brings the next trade. Which leaves the next fee. Which buys the next need.

Content drives fees. Proof drives more. Nobody has to decide to give for the loop to turn, and everyone can see it turning.

What would kill it, and the rule for each

A fake receipt.

Save the Kids, 2021: a coin that promised trading proceeds to children's charities. Insiders sold within days, and an $80,000 donation it claimed to have made was never received. Source.

Every payout is Paid's public reply, not my word. Every purchase is a dated store receipt. Every need has the org's photo. The ledger lives on this site and on X, and rows are never deleted. One fake row would end this, and it should.

Money without a thing.

GiveCrypto, 2018 to 2024: Coinbase's crypto-giving fund wound down after concluding that cash transfers alone didn't produce lasting change once they stopped. Source.

No cash drops. One named item, bought and delivered, every time.

A moment instead of a loop.

Ice Bucket year two. A campaign has an end date. Attention leaves with it.

No campaigns. The next need goes up the day the last one is proven.

A skeptic would say I'm one screenshot away from being Save the Kids with better branding. Correct. That is exactly why the receipts page exists, why it links out instead of asserting, and why it never deletes.
