How it works
01

A nonprofit names one need under $500 and sends one photo.

No wallet, no form, no bank rail, no account. The item shows up.

02

Fees from $GIVE trades accrue and get paid out in dollars.

Paid claims the creator fees on chain and pays 80% to my X Money account as the balance crosses $5, $10, $20, $50, $100, $250, $500, and $1,000. The other 20% is Paid's, spent buying and burning its own token.

That split is Paid's rule for every token on its rail, not mine.

03

I buy the need. The receipt and the photo go public.

Posted, funded, bought, proven. Same four words every time. If the photo is late, the entry says "proof overdue" until it arrives. Nothing is deleted.

Where the money is, at each step
On chain
A trade on $GIVE leaves a creator fee. It sits in the token's fee account until claimed.
Paid
Paid claims it into its treasury, converts it to dollars, and holds the dollars in its own X Money balance until a payout milestone.Their custody, their rules. Their capital-flow page.
Me
The payout lands in my personal X Money account. Paid replies publicly on X with the amount.This is my income. It is not a donation and not tax-deductible for anyone.
The store
I buy the exact item the nonprofit named and post the dated receipt.
The nonprofit
Receives the item, sends a photo of it in use. The entry is marked proven.

Fees first. Donations later.

There is no donor today. That's the design, for now.

Fees fund the needs while the loop gets proven in public. Donations open after that, under a structure built to hold other people's money. Why I think this works, and what would break it, is on the vision page.
