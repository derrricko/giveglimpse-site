Chapter two

A coin whose only job is
to fund things you can see.

A nonprofit names one thing it needs, under $500. Trading fees on $GIVE pay for it. A photo proves it happened.

Every need is a thread on @GiveGlimpse. Posted, funded, bought, proven. If the photo doesn't come, the thread says "proof overdue" until it does.

Nonprofits do one thing

Name a need and send a photo. No wallet, no form, no bank rail, no account. The item shows up.

Fees do the funding

Every trade on $GIVE leaves a small fee behind. Paid routes it to the founder's X Money. Nobody has to decide to give.

X is the ledger

Each payout is confirmed in public by @UsePaid. Each purchase and each photo lands in the need's thread. Nothing hidden, nothing deleted.

One need, four words

Posted. Funded. Bought. Proven.

The same four steps every time, so anyone can read a receipt at a glance.

  1. 01 Posted — the org, the item, the price, a photo of the need.
  2. 02 Funded — the fee payout, quoted from @UsePaid's public reply.
  3. 03 Bought — the store receipt, dated.
  4. 04 Proven — the org's photo of the item in use. Never a face.

The first $1,000

Here is where it goes, in order, as each org says yes.

$100, a starter dignity kit for a kid entering foster care. $100, a literacy kit for an after-school program. $250, a reentry starter kit. $500, bunkbeds for a foster-care supply org. Each becomes a thread the day it's funded.

The honest part

Fees first. Donations later.

Right now there is no donor. Fees from $GIVE come to Derrick Woepking personally through X Money, and he buys the need himself. Donations open once this is proven safe and real, under a structure built for it. Nothing is promised to anyone who holds $GIVE.
